Check-in and doing better

Posted on February 10, 2026 · Category: Personal

It's been a second, but I want to check in and ground myself. A lot has started drifting towards being bad, but some stuff is drifting towards being good. I intend to steer it in the right direction.

Friendships

I have good friends I can talk to that are genuinely a good influence on my life and my mood. I think trying to find time to spend more time around better influences as opposed to bad is important. To try and be better, I should strive to surround myself with people that embody what I want to improve in. It is also about time I branch out. I was somehow such a social butterfly 3 semesters ago. Hopefully I can rekindle at least some of this

Kindness

I feel (partly due to who I surround myself with) that I have become way more cynical and an asshole to an extent. Not just performatively, I know I should just be a kinder person. I talk too much behind peoples backs, I make too many snarky one liners that could and are hurting peoples feelings. Talking to people that don't approach conversations as a chance to make fun of someone is such a welcome thing. It makes me remember that it is very easy to separate toxicity and fun conversations. Sometimes it feels like, "Those are the fun conversations" and that this is "just how ALL people joke around with their friends." Obviously not, you can have fun without being an ass.

I also feel I am a bit of an ass in conversations. I can tell I talk between a little too much to a lot too much depending on the context. Even when I genuinely let people talk and I listen, I still feel I do a bad job of acting engaged (even when I am). I think trying to get to know people better and asking them about themselves is a fun way to talk and get to know someone, while also just being a nicer and more interesting person to talk to.

Obsession

In conversations and my friendships I obsess about the same conversation topics. Especially with talking about classes, there is a time to be passionate, but sometimes I feel I end up in the same stupid conversations. It's not even like I don't have other hobbies, I just don't talk about them. If I care about being a person with breadth and multiple things that define me, I should talk as if this is true. There are probably a large subset of people I interact with that don't know basic things about me, because I obsess over the same things that honestly we don't need to talk about as much as we do.

Reflection

I constantly feel at the crossroads with every decision where I can choose to be better or be worse. I feel recently I have chosen to be worse, due to it being easier or more familiar. Hopefully the opportunities I'll have in this upcoming semester will help me be better, but those opportunities only matter if I take it. In general, I think it is evident from how I write this that I am not satisfied with myself, but I hope to get better!

Self Respect and Relationships

I think what is stopping me from wanting a relationship truly enought to act is that I don't believe I am personally in a good enough spot to even consider this. I think I don't have enough self respect or self love (whatever you want to call it) for my own morality and life. This doesn't mean I should have a bigger ego and think I'm better, this will only make the problem worse. It's more just having the self respect to stand at the crossroads and make the harder decision because I have self respect that this is necessary for me to improve.

To hold myself accountable, I will try to write one of these check-ins weekly

Conclusion

This year could be the greatest year of my life by many metrics, but in a lot of ways could be the worst. I can't change the past at this point, the mistakes I've made can only be lessons to the future. However, I hope I can steer the future back to who I truly am and who I truly strive to be, while being the best version of myself I can!

Fall 2025 Class Review

Posted on December 28, 2025 · Category: Academic

CS61C (5/10)

I am not exactly impressed with the class. My distaste for the class is partially because I had some unreal high expectations for this class. I was told this class would change the way I see the world, but tbh it wasn't that crazy. As much as I "learned" how a computer works, so much of the integral stuff was completely swept under the rug. I respect the fact it is a survey course and it isn't supposed to teach everything, but I do feel a sad void from the fact that it really felt Dan was trying to create this facade that we really did understand the full stack.

Lets talk about the content (the good part). The content was in general interesting and I did learn a lot. Heres each topic:

  • C: uninteresting content in general. Also, snek was a lazy project, as we could have done so much more for dynamic memory management. C also just wasn't really taught, just a vessel to learn other stuff, so it felt overly rushed because of this
  • RISC was also very uninteresting content wise. Classify was also just spamming calling convention for like hours and hours. It was a little interesting to know how assembly actually works, but getting this nitty gritty of calling convention for example yet not delving into the compiler or OS too much feels like a waste, like why go so in depth of how assembly is written but abstract the interesting part
  • CPU was in general very well done lectures wise but it contained the most atrocious project. The idea of designing a CPU from scratch and how they managed to fit it into 32 bit intstructions was mind blowing and cool, but the project being literally copy pasting data lines from premade blocks was very disappointing. I think I learned zero new things from the project as opposed to just watching the lectures
  • Pipelining was short and sweet, well paced, felt interesting without dragging
  • Caches were very useful and practical with very interesting lectures. However, test questions and discussion questions were awful, should've stayed more towards AMAT code questions that actually teach how to write cache efficient code rather than cache size mechanical questions.
  • Parallelism is similar. It was very interesting content wise, but it was kind of stupid enough that they couldn't actually go anywhere with it. Data level parallelism was very boring and the uniform test questions on it displayed this as well. Thread level parallelism couldn't go deep due to us completely abstracting the OS. In general it was very interesting for what it was, but it could've been better
  • Virtual Memory was cool and well paced. It was honestly not the best taught and the questions were too mechanical instead of conceptual.
I know I bashed like half the content, but in general the class holistically gave me decent intuition on a computer so I can't be too mad. It did feel like so much potential was lost though, which I will discuss below.

My main gripe with the class was the pedagogy. For a disclaimer I feel I have a decent claim to talk about this topic. I went to at least 80% of lectures (and do for every class) and I went to every discussion, so I am able to talk about the way the class is run. To start, the lectures were sometimes great, and sometimes nothing burgers. For example, some lectures especially during the single stage CPU felt so engaging and interesting as Dan built the pipeline. However, a lot of lectures like especially in RISC felt like 50% review, 40% tangents, and 10% new content. I think rambling and reiterating is reasonable, however, we didn't even get all the content we needed.

Let's talk my favorite part of the class and the savior of the class. The discussions and videos. Genuinely how is one supposed to do a mechanical cache or vm question without going to discussion or seeing it on a past exam. I swear it was barely taught in lecture or at all. This brings the pain of this course, prairielearn. The homework was honestly reasonable, but the unreasonable part was that I am able to fully teach a lectures content to someone else, yet I don't have the required knowledge to do the homework. I would literally need to either watch the 20 minute guiding video, or I would need to wait for discussion to even start. It just feels like crap to spend time going to lecture just to have to watch more videos anyway because the lecture and homework were so disjoint. To add onto this, office hours were a nightmare. I don't see the vision of staffing office hours with academic interns. Like it can be useful, but a lot of the time they wouldn't know whats going on either. A lot of classes literally put their head TAs in OH constantly and other people that are tested to know everything. It would suck to get stuck on something because it was literally taught nowhere in the entire course and staff didn't know either.

tldr; overall decent class 5/10, but such a missed opportunity due to bad pedagogy.

EE120 (6/10)

EE120 is a class that doesn't sell itself highly and delivers as mediocre as it promises, but then adds some beauty at the end. If you want to learn how to describe a system using fourier analysis and to truly grasp the fourier domain, this is the perfect class for you. If you want some beautiful heavily applicable class that creates you a signals genius, this class will just be a stepping stone to the next one.

The pacing and teaching was just slow. I think this is partially due to at this point 16A and 120 being a little disjoint, so there is a lot of overlap leading to the first 1/3 (but basically 1/2 becuase the first midterm was like halfway through the semester for some reason) of the class being basically nothing new as opposed to 16A (besided convolutions). However, it makes up for it with the back half of the class being way more difficult and interesting.

The good was that it is very useful. I feel I have very very strong intuition on what a fourier transform is and what the s-plane is. I also think the ending where they went into modulation and sampling very very interesting. I think in that way the class was definietly worth my time. A bit of advice though is that this class is 100% a waste of your time if you already are good at fourier stuff and laplace stuff. You can self study everything other than that in the span of like a day if you tried hard enough. In terms of everything else, heres a lightning round

  • Lectures: 2 hours is too long, but Dimakis tried his best with slow content
  • Discussions: reasonable for the block of time they had
  • Labs: shockingly interesting, especially the FFT one and the final lab
  • Hw: reasonable and interesting. As they got to the end they really started to cook (heat equation my beloved)
  • Exams: hard, but in general reasonable. Very mechanical, but it is a math class at the end of the day

tldr; very useful, but don't expect too much

EE105 (9/10)

Wow. Wow. Wow. Kristofer Pister my GOAT. Man single handedly got me through every single technical interview I have ever done up to this point. I genuinely put in so much time into this course because it was so interesting and so useful. I think the foreword of Ali Niknejad's reader describes everything I want to say so I will just quote it throughout, but my god was the reader so useful. It was obvious that Pister pulled from it heavily (so it was super useful in clarifying stuff and more mechanical derivations), but he also used it as a launching off point for other cool stuff. Like why did Pister decide to teach about slew rate of a 2 stage op amp on day 3? Idk but it's so useful and so interesting. Also, like why did Niknejad guest lecture and derive a 2T2R op amp off of a CD CG two stage amplifier? I guess he just did.

The best way to sum up the way the class is taught is love of the game. Here is Niknejad's words not mine "I assure you that the material in this book is difficult to learn and will involve many sleepless nights and many frustrating days when you just don’t understand something. If you love this stuff, I also know that you won’t give up. That’s what my students teach me every year." Truly absolute cinema. He is right. I basically went from a PCB monkey who just knew circuits from KCL and KVL, to someone who can reasonably see a situation with complicated device setups and pinpoint a lot of small details. Obviously there are core things I still don't get, but 105 is the launching point to everything else.

Speaking of which, the content. I appreciate the first week of the course. It really felt like Pister was saying, here is everything you know, and here is exactly how we can abuse it and rigorize it. For example, he just showed a boost converter off just to test intuition of time domain inductors, and he showed s-plane plots to show how resonance works and how phase and magnitude of signals relate. He then went into op amps, god bless, which was just a fun way of making all of us better electrical engineers. Then the fun part of the class, devices! Here is what Niknejad has to say " Many other well known books cover similar grounds, but compromise on one front or another. For example, most device physics books don’t delve into the depths of analog circuit design, and most circuit analysis and design books don’t bother to teach the device physics with a solid foundation, sweeping many important details under the rug, so to speak, or just not addressing the physics at all. In this book, as well as the accompanying lecture slides, I am asking a lot of my students students as I am attempting to bring them up with a solid foundation on all three fronts." I think this is why the class is special. I really felt like I have enough physics background to abstract away and get decent intuition on devices, while also managing to keep the class rooted in the circuits that we will eventually design with these devices.

Why a 9/10 and not a 10/10. The class was excrutiatingly time consuming. This was in a lot of ways a good thing. Content this dense that brings people from basically knowing nothing to being able to understand most analog circuits today isn't easy. The homework in turn really demanded that we truly learn the content. I would be lying though if I said it wasn't a pain, as sometimes we would be up until like 4:00 am just to finish an assignment because of how thick the homework ended up being. I enjoyed Pister putting datasheet and paper reading into homework as it was truly interesting, but it was hit or miss. Some were very interesting, and some really did feel like a waste of time.

The labs :( so many things I could say. There was indeed magic in the labs. Seeing all the curves we derived on the conceptual side on the parameter analyzer was very cool. Seeing things like the photodiodes and the op amp work were also incredible. I also can't stress how great the lab staff were. They were doomed with infinite work and they were still merciful on us. However, the labs might have been too long. Like a lot too long. Like we were there for 10+ hours too long. 9 labs is a lot of labs shockingly. Especially when they last as long as they did.

tldr; crippling amount of work for easily the most important class and content I have learned at Berkeley so far!

To go on a tangent, I constantly fight with my ego. Prestigous degree programs at colleges have got to be the greatest feedback loop of ego feeding I've ever witnessed. I think I've lost that. This semester has been a rollercoaster of being really busy, and really caring about applying to places for the first time in a while. "Don't Compare" is the greatest advice of all time, and I can't help but notice I compare a lot lately.

So why am I talking about comparing myself to other EECS majors in a blog post about an acapella performance? Sitting there smiling I couldn't help but feel like I should join something like this. My Current Oxymoronic Music Taste

Posted on December 1, 2025 · Category: Entertainment

I just went to see one of my favorite bands Of Monsters and Men play live at the Fox Theater, so I want to kinda reflect on my changing music taste and how I can learn more about myself from it. I have slowly been drifting away from more traditional sounds in alternative music from say: The Strokes, Arctic Monkeys, The Clash, Paramore, Pixies, etc. to more experimental and "odd" sounds. Whether this is for better or for worse I can't say, but it is interesting. The bands in question I listen to these days are: Pink Floyd, Bjork, Sonic Youth, Of Monsters and Men. I will go through each.

Me and Pink Floyd this summer have a fun relationship. I kinda started off listening to Dark Side like any normal person, finding my way to Animals, then eventually The Wall. Favorite songs are: Brain Damage, Mother, and Pigs. What does this say? I could go on about this or that, but I think it boils down to the fact that I am at a very rough part of my life mentally (so these songs help to cope), and also that I just love prog rock more than ever.

Bjork I started listening to through The Sugarcubes. Favorite songs are: Hidden Place and Isobel. I think Bjork has a very special type of introspection and escapism with her music that I've been gravitating towards. Maybe it's just the raw emotion Bjork has, but I guess even though the music itself isn't that warm and cozy (often quite the opposite through dissonance), I do feel a sense of comfort while listening. Additionally, her music might just be that good.

Sonic Youth I've been listening to for a while, but only now really digging into all of their albums. My favorite songs are: Teen Age Riot, Schizophrenia, and Superstar. Similarly with Bjork, I feel an odd comfort in their music. In a weird way, something inside me makes me really want to lash out and destroy the world around me and change it. Teen Age Riot (as well as a lot of others especially on Daydream Nation) gives me that outlet. Schizophrenia and Superstar are two songs that I honestly shouldn't feel so emotionally attached to (as they are honestly very depressing). Alas, the comfort is there. It lets me sulk in my sadness without it affecting the rest of my life.

Of Monsters and Men is the band that has probably influence my life the most out of all of these. I could write for hours on all of their songs I love (and I even have written a research paper on them) but thats not what I want to talk about. At the Of Monsters and Men concert, I couldn't help but smile. Why? There are probably infinite reasons, but why even care? If it makes me feel happy, it just does. Nevermind the songs being genuinely depressing lyrically, the comfort of hearing the band sing with such emotion overpowers any of that. You are just left overcoming these demons together.

Music that is depressing and heavy can paradoxically have the opposite effect. Who would've thought.

Recent Media I've Consumed - Purpose

Posted on November 17, 2025 · Category: Entertainment

I've consumed a good bit of new media recently that has got me thinking a lot. For context, I am a big fan of existentialist media. Not saying I am a nihilist and my world view revolves around lack of meaning. I just find the godless view of an absurd reality a very fascinating lens to view the world through. Examples of this include "The Stranger" which I find to be a unique and interesting interpretation of how meaning exists and doesn't in peoples lives.

The first thing I've recently watched is Fight Club, which honestly has been a long time coming. As a disclaimer, I don't believe Tyler in the film is correct and I do believe the actions that took place are wrong on many accounts. However, I think the way the film portrays meaning in the world very interesting, both from the lens of an existentialist and from the lens of a man living in the 21st century. Because I could probably write essays on this movie if I sat here long enough, I just want to talk about the idea of possessions in the film. I'm not talking necessarily about the anti-capitalist view of the film, but moreso the idea that many people view purpose as having the most money or owning the most things. The film is very quick to point out how sad defining your personality on your own possessions is. This is something that, whether I try to avoid it or not, fall into myself. It is very easy to (let's say applying to things as example), just consider what the positions status and pay will give you, while forgetting that at the end of the day it is YOUR life you are determining. I also enjoy in fight club how they show the flip side of this argument. The belief that one system is wrong and the right solution is just to remove the wrong system is just inherently flawed. Like how is destroying capitalism going to bring the protagonist any happiness. It feels very easy to pretend that it will help, but this reminds you it isn't that simple. All in all, very entertaining and interesting movie.

I also recently read the short play "No Exit" by Sartre. Without dwelling on it (because again this is too much to say), the idea of how purpose is defined is also interesting here. "No Exit" determines a persons self worth in terms of how others percieve your self worth. The characters truly feel they are good or bad because of what others see them as. The perception of oneself is the perception of everyones perception. I would love to try and pretend in my life that this doesn't affect me, but all I can really do is try to care less. Funny enough, this theme and ideas mirror the anime Evangelion, where they greatly describe the concept that suffering comes because of the different versions of a single person that exists due to how they are differently percieved by people. In general, it is a good reminder to try and be less anxious about your own self image and to be more genuine. I have also recently ordered "Nausea" by Sartre and am interested in to see if my opinions change.

The last one is kind of funny. I gaslit myself to watch the entirety of "Chainsaw Man" because my friends were watching the movie in a day. I think there was a lot good, but also a lot bad. However, I want to specifically talk about the way the show talks about the absurd purposes the protagonists in the show possess. Throughout season one of the show, you witness the classic character tropes of their purpose being tied to something noble, such as protecting their family or getting revenge. However, two of the main characters, Denji and Power, have very absurd motives. What really captivated me was a dialogue throughout the anime about Denji asking why his lustful motives are any less valid than another characters noble motives. It was a funny scene with the audience getting to laugh at how absurd Denji is, but it is also a good question. Why is his motive any less reasonable than anyone elses? Like I can obviously have the opinion it is stupid, but that is just my opinion. If his passion towards that motive is just as strong as my passion for something else, what position am I in to say that he is wrong. My reading of this is that you really can't. As long as the motivation is truly authentic, you can judge, but you can't fault someone for believing this. Thinking about this is, funny enough, very liberating. It feels easy to feel purpose is X, and Y is dumb. But if I truly believe Y is my calling, as long as I mean it authentically, it is just as valid as anything else.

In conclusion, purpose is fluid and far from predetermined by anything!

CALICO Fall 2025

Posted on November 14, 2025 · Category: Club

CALICO Fall 2025 wrapped up this weekend and what a semester it has been so far for CALICO. This club in all honesty was fated to die during our Fall 2024 contest, and two contests later we are still here and still growing. For context, CALICO was founded by two people that have since graduated, making the entire officer board people that want to keep the club alive out of love for the club. For even more context, we ended the Spring 2025 contest on a high note of our biggest contest so far up to that point, but on a low note of over half the club graduating. This meant we literally were left with give or take 5 dedicated members, not enough to run a whole 2000+ person contest. So how did we perservere?

A lot of recruiting. When I say a lot of recruiting I mean a lot. Me and my co-director literally put in 2-4 hour daily shifts of tabling (not even including transport time of bringing tabling supplies all the way across campus) for the first 2-3 weeks of the semester. We then geared the first 2 meetings reinventing the way the club onboards and gets members involed, as historically the club has never recruited at this scale. This included way more interactive first few meetings, such as our writing a Rank 1 problem in one day event. As of writing this post contest, we had ~20 members show up day of the contest to help, and ~10 highly dedicated members in our team.

CALICO is something so special to me for such intangible reasons. For all things considered, I shouldn't be the director of this club. I am someone interested in low level hardware and embedded systems, not someone fated to be a leetcode grinding SWE. Yet here I am "reviving" a software club and helping carry it on past our 10th contest of Fall 2025. The nature of a club like CALICO is that we don't look super good on resumes compared to clubs like consulting clubs, and we don't really pay members (outisde our free food at meetings), so we can really only rely on people being there because they want to be there. Me and my co-director literally had a serious conversation of our procedure of disbanding the club because we really didn't know what to do if we didn't recruit well enough. Yet here we are, finding more people who love hosting this silly competition with us.

I don't know if I will be the "director" of another CALICO (don't worry I will still be a big player next semester, but maybe not as full fledged director), but I do want to say how much this club means to people. CALICO is a community. I mean this both in the corny club way of these people in the club literally being my friends, but also in the fact we have an entire community discord who await our next contest. I stepped up to direct not because I felt CALICO director was my ticket to a software internship, but because I couldn't watch this community I love so much die. From our socials, to retreats, to us just talking about random stuff during meetings, I hope CALICO and the spirit of its community lives on. I really can't imagine my Berkeley experience any other way. Viva La CALICO!

Thanks,

Your CALICO Fall 2025 Director
